# The daemon closes a remote client that stops answering: a pane after three unanswered probes, a service stream after 60 s with nothing from the app

Proposed 2026-11-13, from the Railway pane-attach design interview; amends [ADR 0070](0070-remote-attaches-are-kept-alive-by-the-panes-terminal.md). It is accepted once a Railway host shows both kinds of connection closed, and their `relay` and durable session ended, about a minute after the Mac holding them is cut off.

ADR 0070 has a remote pane's client that misses three keepalive probes stop counting as attached, but not cut off: it keeps its connection and its output, and the relay under it lives until sshd notices the dead link, about 2 h on stock Linux. Railway's relay never notices. The spike of 2026-11-13 found that every login through it is a durable session whose process keeps running after its client is gone, with nothing that ends it but the process exiting, so an abandoned pane's `relay` (ADR 0074) or the app's service stream would run as long as the sandbox, with the daemon still writing to it.

So the daemon closes the connection itself. A client that opted into keepalive (`WORKROOM_SESSION_KEEPALIVE=1`, remote panes only) and misses three probes, about 45 to 60 s, is closed, not only marked stale; its `relay` then has nothing to talk to and exits, which ends a Railway durable session, and on any other host ends the process sshd would have kept. The app pings every remote service stream every 15 s, and the daemon closes a remote service connection that has sent nothing for 60 s. Only a connection whose handshake says it pings gets that deadline, so an older app, and every local connection, never does. A Mac that comes back has lost its ssh anyway (`ServerAliveCountMax` gives up after about 45 s) and reconnects, and its sessions are reattached as usual, since a session outlives every client. This holds on every remote host, not only Railway.

Rejected: killing an abandoned login from a second ssh login, which the spike showed works on Railway. It depends on Railway's session list, a private interface, and the daemon already knows which of its clients stopped answering. Rejected: keeping ADR 0070's mark-only rule off Railway. An abandoned client there costs an idle process and output written to nobody for hours, for no benefit.
