# One switcher shows the sidebar's Open or Projects view, and the docked sidebar is flat

Decided 2026-11-26 (owner, mock-up option D for #819). Status: accepted. Amends ADR 0082: the Open section and the Projects section are no longer two folding sections stacked in the sidebar; they are the sidebar's **Open view** and **Projects view**, one shown at a time.

ADR 0082 put the Open section above the Projects section, each a sticky folding heading over its own pane, with a draggable divider between them. With both open, neither had room: the Open rows pushed the project tree down, and the divider and two folds were three controls for one question, which list do I want to look at now. The sidebar now answers it with one control.

- **One switcher, one view.** A bar at the top of the sidebar has two tabs, each a glyph alone with its name as its tooltip: Open, a terminal, and Projects, a 2×2 grid of squares. The shown view's tab is in the primary colour, over a 2pt accent underline on the bar's hairline; the other is secondary. Both glyphs keep one weight, and a hidden view's attention dot overlays the gap beside its glyph, so neither tab moves as the selection or the dot changes. Below the bar is the shown view's list, filling the sidebar and scrolling itself, with the notification footer under it. Only that view is mounted, so the hidden one's rows neither run nor stay on screen as drop zones. There are no section headings, folds, pane weights or divider any more; old saved values for them are ignored.
- **The "+" acts on the shown view.** In Open it is the new-terminal menu (New Terminal, New Terminal Group…); opening a workroom is ⌘O's (File ▸ Open Workroom…), so the menu doesn't repeat it (amended 2026-11-26). In Projects it is Add Project. With no projects the Projects view shows "No projects yet" and Add Project…, under the switcher.
- **A dot says the hidden view needs you.** While a view is hidden, its tab carries a 6pt accent dot when something in it waits on the user: a target with unread notifications or a terminal waiting for input, read from what the rows read. For Open that is the window's workroom tabs; for Projects, every project's root and workrooms. The two overlap, since an open workroom is in both.
- **The selection is the window's.** Each window keeps which view it shows, saved and restored with its session as its Open rows' folds are. A new window shows Open. View ▸ Sidebar ▸ Open Tabs and Projects pick a view (the Open view is called Open Tabs wherever users see it, since "Open" alone reads as a verb; amended 2026-11-27) and show a hidden sidebar (the submenu also holds Show Sidebar, ⌃⌘S; amended 2026-11-26, when View ▸ Inspector became a submenu too); their default keys are ⌃⌘T and ⌃⌘P, beside Show Sidebar's ⌃⌘S, because ⌘1 and ⌘2 switch workroom tabs.
- **Drags reach either view.** A terminal's tab let go on the Open tab becomes a standalone terminal, as on the old Open heading. A terminal drag that rests on the hidden view's tab for half a second shows that view, so a drop can land in it. A drag that starts on a Projects-tree terminal row does not spring, since showing Open would end it.
- **The docked sidebar is a flat column.** No rounded card, border, shadow or margins: it sits flush to the window's leading edge, under the title bar and over the window footer, on the window frame's own colour (`ChromeBackground.bar`, the title bar's and footer's), with no edge of its own: it reads as part of the window, not a panel on it. The edge-reveal panel keeps its floating card, with the same switcher and views inside.

Rejected: keeping both sections and adding the switcher as a shortcut to fold one (two ways to do one thing, and the divider would stay). Rejected: tabs that keep both lists mounted to keep each one's scroll position (the hidden list would keep running its rows and its drop zones would overlap the shown one's). Rejected: binding Show Open and Show Projects to ⌘1 and ⌘2 (they are Switch to Workroom Tab 1 and 2, which the title bar and the keyboard rely on).
